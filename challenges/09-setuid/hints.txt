The program needs to run as its owner, `vaultadm`, instead of as
whoever launched it. There is one permission bit that does exactly
that.

    ls -l /opt/lab/setuid/bin/checkpw
---
It is the "set user id" bit. When it is on, the program runs with
the identity of its owner, no matter who started it.

In `ls -l` it shows up as an `s` where the owner's `x` normally
is: `-rwsr-xr-x`.
---
Set it with `u+s`, or with a `4` as a fourth digit in front of the
mode. The program is currently `755`.

Do not touch `passwords.txt`. That is the whole point -- you are
not gaining the permission, the program is.
---
    sudo chmod u+s /opt/lab/setuid/bin/checkpw

(equivalently `sudo chmod 4755 ...`)

Now `checkpw niko hunter2` runs as `vaultadm`, which *can* open the
store -- while you still cannot `cat` it yourself.
